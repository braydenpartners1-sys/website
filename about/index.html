About Brayden Partners

Brayden Partners works with organisations that need to establish, strengthen or rethink how their people and business operations are managed.

Our work sits at the intersection of HR, business operations and technology, helping organisations address the structures and processes behind their day-to-day activities.

Businesses change. Their ways of working need to change too.

The arrangements that work for a small team don’t always work as the organisation grows.

Responsibilities become less clear. Processes develop differently across departments. Managers rely on informal arrangements, and information becomes harder to manage.

These problems can affect recruitment, workforce management, operational decisions and the experience of employees.

Brayden was established to help organisations address these challenges by developing clearer structures, more reliable processes and appropriate systems.

We believe good business infrastructure should make work easier to manage, not create another layer of complexity.

Experience across HR and business operations.

  • MBA
  • MSc in Financial Management
  • CIPD Level 5

Brayden Partners is led by its founder, an HR and business operations professional with experience across financial services, education and organisational development.

That career has included building people management infrastructure in growing businesses, leading operational functions, supporting organisational restructuring and improving the systems used to manage workforce information and processes.

At Moneda Invest, the role developed from people management into wider business operations, giving direct experience of the relationship between organisational growth, workforce requirements and operational decision-making.

Work in UK education has included HR operations, recruitment, organisational change, workforce administration and management support.

Brayden operates as a founder-led consultancy, drawing on specialists in technology, law and HR where engagements require additional expertise.

What shapes our work.

Understand the organisation.

Before changing a process, we need to understand why it exists, who uses it and where it creates difficulties.

Consider the wider business.

HR decisions affect costs, managers, employees and operations. Solutions need to account for those relationships.

Make responsibilities clear.

Processes work better when people know what they are responsible for, what decisions they can make and when they need support.

Keep things manageable.

An organisation should be able to operate and maintain its processes without depending indefinitely on external support.
